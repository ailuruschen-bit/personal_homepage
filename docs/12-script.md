# 12 Script

**Status: proposed, and the lines are drafts.** This is the shooting script for
the site: what the camera does, what is on screen, and what is said, scene by
scene. [10](10-scroll-narrative.md) defines the camera; [11](11-premise-and-voice.md)
defines the premise and the rules the writing has to obey. This page is where
they meet.

When the lines are final they move into `content/`, and this page keeps only
the structure.

## Conventions

The prose is English, like every other page here. **The lines themselves are
Chinese**, because they are content rather than documentation — the same rule
that kept the interface copy and the signage in the artwork untranslated.

```
[A]  anchored   a function of scroll offset; replays on a second pass
[R]  reactive   answers the pointer or idling; may never play at all
[L]  live       only in the confrontation, and only once
[   ] placeholder — needs a real fact and is not mine to invent
```

## Cast

| Who      | What they are                                                       |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 侦探     | the reader. Heard, never seen. Every `[A]` and `[R]` line is his.   |
| 他       | the occupant. Appears once, in a doorway, as a silhouette.          |
| the tape | the third character: the timecode, the grain, the fact of playback. |

## The setting

A night train, somewhere between two cities, after midnight. One sleeper
compartment with the light left on.

The occupant has stepped out — to the dining car, the vestibule, a phone call;
it is never said. The detective lets himself in **through the connecting door
from the next carriage**, which is the only way into a compartment on a moving
train and the reason nobody climbs through a window.

What the reader is watching is **the recording of that intrusion**, which is why
it can be rewound. See [11](11-premise-and-voice.md#the-recording).

## Scene 0 — The tape begins

**Segment:** the opening, locked. **Camera:** as built today — the blinds tilt
open, the camera pushes through the window and out to the city.

No new artwork. What changes is only what it means: he has just come in, and
the first thing he does is check where the train is.

```
[A] 这种锁，十年前就该换了。
[A] 咖啡还冒着气。他以为自己一分钟就回来。
```

The first line is the whole character in nine words: he is not impressed, he
has seen this lock before, and he is mildly offended on behalf of the railway.
The second is the only clock the site ever puts on screen, and it is a line of
dialogue rather than a mechanic. Nothing counts down.

It also plants a motif that pays off in scene 6.

## Scene 1 — The window

**Segment:** cover, `s` 0 → 240. **Camera:** outside on the city, parallax
only. **On screen:** the city, the rain, the timecode.

```
[A] 景色不错。
[A] 可惜没人花钱请我看风景。
```

Two beats rather than one line, anchored a little apart, so the scroll itself
performs the turn from looking to working. The second is also the scroll
affordance: it says what happens next. Keep a quiet scroll indicator anyway.

## Scene 2 — The carriage

**Segment:** porch, window transit inward, hub. **Camera:** the existing
transit, landing on the hub framing. **On screen:** the compartment, pointer
focus live.

The transit is a glance away and back, not an entrance. He is already inside.

```
[A] 一个人的铺位。东西摆得像随时准备跑路。
[A] 这种人我见得多了。要么是赶时间，要么是心里有鬼。

[R] 车窗    外面还是那片城市。它不会开口。
[R] 电脑    屏幕还亮着。现在的人连锁屏都懒得按。
[R] 公文包  没上锁。要么是心大，要么是没货。
[R] 车票    一张票能说的，比一个人自己说的多。
[R] idle    我站着也不会有人替我翻。
[R] 看过的  又是它。我记性还没差到那个地步。
```

The idle line is doing interface work: it is the only nudge the site gives a
reader who has stopped without meaning to, and it nudges in character instead
of putting a hint on screen.

## Scene 3 — 车票 (about)

**Segment:** chapter 1. **Camera:** carry — it comes up off the table to the
middle of the frame.

**Artifact:** the face of the ticket. `[姓名]` · `[出发地] → [目的地]` ·
`[日期]`.

**Annotation:** who he is, in the report voice, written after the fact.

```
[A] open   一张票，折过两次，边上起毛了。在口袋里待过很长时间。
[A] close  位置差一厘米他都看得出来。这种人我惹不起。
```

The close is a compliment wearing a complaint. It is also the first time the
detective concedes anything to the occupant, which is worth spending early.

## Scene 4 — 线路图 (journey)

**Segment:** chapter 2. **Camera:** dolly — it is screwed to the wall.

**Artifact:** the line, with stations. A route map is already a timeline on a
wall, which is why this chapter needs no translating: the stops are `[经历]`.

**Annotation:** where he has been, and what he was doing there.

```
[A] open   墙上钉着线路图。有几站被圈了起来，而且圈了不止一次。
[A] close  一个随身带着自己走过的路的人。要么很念旧，要么还没走到地方。
```

"圈了不止一次" is the whole chapter in five characters: he came back to it. The
close offers two readings and picks neither, which is what keeps it from
becoming a caption.

## Scene 5 — 公文包 (stack)

**Segment:** chapter 3. **Camera:** carry — down off the luggage rack, lid
open, and the interior is the aperture.

**Artifact:** what he carries. `[技术栈]`, laid out like tools rather than
listed like a CV.

```
[A] open   没上锁。打开之前我总盼着里面有点刺激的。
[A] close  工具，摆得整整齐齐。这人要是干我这行，早就发财了。
```

A joke with the reading of the whole chapter between its setup and its payoff.
The gap is the point — the longer the stack section runs, the better the close
lands.

## Scene 6 — 电脑 (projects)

**Segment:** chapter 4. **Camera:** dolly — the screen rect becomes the panel.

**Artifact:** what is still open on it. `[项目]`.

```
[A] open   屏幕没锁，连睡眠都没进。他是真的以为自己一分钟就回来。
[A] close  我看不懂他在写什么。但看得懂一个人半夜还在写。
```

The open calls back to scene 0 and closes the motif.

**The close is the one sincere line in the site, and it should stay the only
one.** Everything before it has been dry, so it lands; and it says a thing
about the occupant that the occupant could not say about himself without
sounding like he was asking for credit. That asymmetry is the entire reason for
writing the site in someone else's voice. Spend it once, here.

## Scene 7 — 笔记 and 兴趣

**Blocked on artwork.** No object, no aperture, no chapter — see
[11](11-premise-and-voice.md#the-evidence-map). When they exist they sit
between scenes 6 and 8, and they are the two chapters where the detective
should be least sure what he is looking at. A veteran who has just been moved
by a laptop screen needs to be wrong about something immediately afterwards.

## Scene 8 — The door

**Segment:** the last chapter. **Camera:** the only cut-in in the whole site —
the camera turns to the connecting door as it opens.

He is backlit from the corridor and is a silhouette. He is never drawn further
than that.

```
[A] 门响了。
[A] 干这行学会的第一件事：门响的时候，别回头。
[A] ……我还是回头了。
```

Three beats, and the third is what turns the camera. The site's only cut-in is
motivated by a joke at the detective's own expense, which is cheaper and better
than motivating it with suspense.

Then the tape chrome goes. The timecode stops, the indicator goes out, the
grain settles, the subtitle loses its transcript styling. Playback has caught
up with the present, and what follows is live.

```
[L] 他  ……你坐错车厢了吧？
```

Understatement rather than a shout. It is more unsettling, it is in character
for a man who keeps his ticket to the millimetre, and it hands the reader an
obvious thing to say back — which matters, because this line is the chat
section's opening prompt and a conversation that starts with an accusation
starts badly.

Everything after it is the conversation. He asks what the reader has been
reading. The reader can ask him anything back and gets a straight answer.

He fills one doorway; the far end of the carriage is open the whole time.
Leaving is always available, which is what makes staying a choice.

## Scene 9 — The last frame

**Segment:** porch out, window transit outward, departure.

However the conversation goes, it ends on the window: the camera pulls back
through it to the city, which is the frame the tape opened on. The footer lives
out here — links, colophon, source — as a place rather than a strip at the
bottom of a page.

```
[A] 下一趟车我买票。
```

A draft, and replaceable. It works because it is the only moment the detective
admits the whole thing was a break-in, and he admits it as a resolution to do
better rather than an apology.

Nineteen anchored, six reactive. That is over the flat budget of twelve to
fifteen written in [11](11-premise-and-voice.md#budget), and the budget was
measured in the wrong unit — see the note there. Anchored lines are cheap: at
most two to a chapter, never repeating inside one pass. Reactive lines are the
expensive ones, because the hub is where a reader lingers, and six is the
ceiling.

### Devices in use

Worth naming, so a future line is either using one or knows it is not:

- **The turn.** Observation, then a short sentence that undercuts it. Lines 2,
  9, 14, 18.
- **The callback.** "以为自己一分钟就回来" in lines 2 and 19, closed out by the
  thing it turns out to mean.
- **The delayed payoff.** A setup at a chapter's open and its punchline at the
  close, with the whole chapter in between. Lines 17 and 18.
- **Competence as boredom.** He is never excited. The comedy is the gap between
  how dramatic breaking in is and how routine he finds it. Line 1 sets it.
- **One sincere line.** Line 20, and nothing else. See scene 6.

## What is still mine to write and what is not

Everything in brackets needs a real fact: the ticket's fields, the stations on
the map, what is in the briefcase, what is on the screen, and the closing line.
The annotation layer for each chapter is not drafted here at all — it is the
longest writing in the project and the most personal, and it is the part that
has to sound like one person rather than like a device.

The order to write in, from [11](11-premise-and-voice.md#budget): `facts`
first, then the artifact fields, then these anchored lines, then the annotation,
then the reactive lines, then the confrontation's persona.
